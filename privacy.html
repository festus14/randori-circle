Privacy Policy

Last updated: 22 October 2026

In short:

  • Randori Circle is a free, invite-only tool for practising coding interviews. We collect what we need to run it: your account, your circle, and what you do in your pairing rooms.
  • We don’t sell your information or show ads, and we don’t use analytics or advertising cookies. We use Sentry to find errors (see Service providers).
  • Google sign-in gives us only your name, your email address and a Google account ID.
  • Your pairing room is shared with your partner for that week, not with the rest of the circle.
  • You can delete your account yourself in Edit profile, or ask us to delete it at any time.

Who we are

Randori Circle (“Randori”, “we”, “us”) is a free, non-commercial community tool for practising coding interviews. Randori Circle is run by Festus Omole, based in the United Kingdom. As the operator, Festus Omole is responsible for your information (the “data controller” under UK data protection law). See Contact for how to reach us.

Circle owners are members who run a circle. They can’t delete accounts themselves, but can remove you from the circle and pass your request on.

What we collect and why

Your account

  • Email address, to sign you in and to send you Randori’s emails.
  • Name and avatar colour, shown to the members of your circle.
  • Password, if you use one. We store only a bcrypt hash, never the password itself.
  • Profile details you choose to add: a short bio, your time zone, your interview focus and a LeetCode username. These are shown to the members of your circle. We never contact LeetCode; the username is only displayed.
  • When you last signed in, and whether you are an app admin.

Your circle

  • Which circles you belong to, your role (owner or member), who invited you, when you joined, and whether your membership is active.
  • Invitations. When an owner invites someone, we store the invited email address only as a one-way fingerprint (a hash), plus an encrypted copy used to send the invitation email. The encrypted copy is kept with our email records while the invitation can still be used, and is then removed on the same schedule as other email records (see How long we keep it). Invitation links expire after 7 days.
  • Requests to join. If you ask for an invite on our home page, the circle owners see your name, email and note so they can decide.

Your practice

  • Your weekly availability, your pairings, the session times you propose and agree, and a meeting link if your pair adds one.
  • What you write in a pairing room: the shared code editor, the whiteboard and chat messages with your partner.
  • Code runs: the code you run, the exercise and language, the test results and how long it took.
  • Session details: interviewer and candidate roles, the timer, and whether you both marked the session as done.
  • Your email reminder setting. Unless you turn it off, we email you about your pairings and session times, and send reminders: about 24 hours before the Sunday availability cutoff if you haven’t chosen for the next cycle, 3 days after pairing if your pair hasn’t agreed a session time, and a day and about an hour before an agreed session.

Technical information

  • A sign-in cookie (see Cookies and browser storage).
  • Error logs. When something goes wrong while you are signed in, the app sends a short error report to our server. Our server also logs some events, such as code runs. Each log entry can include the page path, your user ID, and the name and main version of your browser and operating system (for example “Chrome 131 on Windows 10/11”). We don’t store your IP address or your browser’s full user agent. Entries linked to your account carry no form of your IP address at all. A few server entries that aren’t linked to any account, such as failed requests, keep a pseudonymous code made from the IP address with a secret key and the date, so we can spot a flood of requests from one place. The code changes every day, but because we hold the key we could still work out which address it came from. Error reports sent from your browser have email addresses, passwords, secret links, IP addresses and browser details removed before they are stored. Our server’s own log entries can also include technical error details and, for code runs, the exercise, language, results and timing, but not your email address or password.
  • To stop abuse, we count sign-in and invitation attempts using a scrambled (keyed hash) form of your IP address, not the address itself.

Google sign-in

If you sign in with Google, Randori asks Google only for the standard openid email profile permissions. From these we use:

  • your Google account ID, so we recognise you next time;
  • your email address, and the fact that Google has verified it;
  • your name.

Google also offers your profile photo; we don’t use or store it. We don’t ask for access to anything else in your Google account, such as Gmail, Drive, Calendar or contacts. We don’t keep Google access tokens or refresh tokens: we check the signed sign-in token Google sends us and then discard it.

We use this information only to sign you in, to show your name and email in Randori, and to send Randori’s own emails to that address. Each time you sign in with Google, your display name is reset to your Google name, or, if Google doesn’t send a name, to the part of your email address before the @. Members of your circle see this display name.

Randori’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Information we receive from Google is not sold, not used for advertising, and not transferred to anyone except as needed to run the service (for example, it is stored with our hosting and database providers) or when the law requires it.

Randori doesn’t keep ongoing access to your Google account. You can remove it from the third-party connections page of your Google Account; if you sign in with Google again, Google will ask you to approve Randori again. Removing it does not delete your Randori account.

Who can see your information

  • Members of your circle see your display name, colour, profile details, whether you are available this week, and who is paired with whom each week. They don’t see your email address.
  • Your partner for the week sees what happens in your pairing room: the shared editor, whiteboard, chat, code runs and results, the session times you propose and agree, the meeting link and whether the session is done. Your sessions stay private to the two of you, except that practice suggestions show your partner which exercises you have attempted (see below).
  • Circle owners see who is in the circle (without email addresses) and the name, email and note of anyone asking to join.
  • Practice suggestions. Your current partner sees which exercises you have attempted before (from your verified code runs and completed sessions, including solo practice and earlier partners), shown only as an “attempted” mark, never your code, scores or dates. Runs in your current shared room count toward both partners’ suggestions; your partner already sees those runs in the room’s run feed. Exercises you hide from suggestions are remembered only in your browser.
  • Circle health. Circle owners see counts for each finished week of their circle: how many members were available, paired or practised solo, how many pairs agreed a time and completed a session, and what share of pairs repeated a recent pairing. Pair counts are hidden in weeks with fewer than four pairs and whenever they could point to a single pair. The counts never name anyone, but a count of all pairs or none does show what every pair did.
  • App admins can read the error logs, including browser and operating system names and, for entries not linked to an account, the daily pseudonymous address code, to fix problems.
  • The people who run Randori can access the database when needed to operate, secure and fix the service.

We don’t sell or share your information with anyone else, except the service providers below or where the law requires it.

Service providers

These companies process information on our behalf to run Randori:

  • Vercel hosts the website: it serves the pages from its global network and runs our server code in its Dublin, Ireland region. It handles every request, including your IP address, and keeps short-lived request logs.
  • Turso hosts our database.
  • Resend sends our emails: invitations, email verification, password resets, pairing and session-time notifications, reminders, and notices to circle owners about requests to join. It receives your email address and the email content, such as your partner’s name, a session time or a room link.
  • Sentry helps us find errors. When you open the app, your browser downloads Sentry’s error-reporting script from Sentry’s servers, so Sentry sees your IP address and browser details. Error reports from your browser can include the page address, browser details, a description of the error and a short trail of what happened just before it (such as pages opened, buttons pressed and the addresses of requests the app made); we don’t attach your name or email, and Sentry is set not to collect personal data by default. Reports from our server have email addresses, user IDs, cookies and code removed. We don’t use Sentry’s session recording or performance tracing in your browser.
  • The code runner is either Vercel Sandbox (a throwaway, isolated machine with no network access, deleted after each run) or a code runner we host ourselves. It receives only the code being run and the test inputs, never your name or email.
  • Google handles Google sign-in. Google’s public STUN servers also help two browsers find each other for a video call, so they see your IP address when you join a call.
  • cdnjs (Cloudflare), jsDelivr and unpkg deliver the code editor and the code formatter to your browser when you open the editor or format code. They see your IP address and browser details.
  • GitHub hosts our code and public issue tracker and runs our scheduled maintenance jobs. A weekly backup check reads the database on GitHub’s servers to confirm our backups can be restored; it keeps only counts and checksums, not your information.
  • Groq, with OpenAI as a backup, would provide optional AI feedback on a practice session. This feature is off. If it is ever turned on, it needs consent from everyone in the room first, and it sends the session notes, code and interviewer questions, but not your name or email. The notes and feedback would be kept while your account exists.

Some of these providers are based in the United States, so your information may be processed outside the UK under their own data protection terms.

Video calls go directly between your browser and your partner’s. The audio and video don’t pass through our servers, and nothing is recorded. The short messages that set up a call, which include network addresses, your display name and a random call ID stored in your browser, pass through our server and are cleared out after about an hour (the clean-up runs while calls are in use).

How long we keep it

  • Your account and practice history (profile, circle membership, availability, pairings, session times, chat, code runs, completed sessions and settings): while your account exists. If you leave a circle or an owner removes you, your membership becomes inactive; you are signed out only if it was your last active circle. Nothing is deleted unless you delete your account or ask us to.
  • Shared editor drafts: deleted about 90 days after the last change. Whiteboards: usually deleted about 90 days after the last change; some whiteboards can be kept for several months longer.
  • Meeting links: removed when your agreed session time changes.
  • Video call set-up messages: about an hour, cleared while calls are in use.
  • Error logs: 30 days. Entries from before we stopped storing IP addresses and full user agents are deleted on the same 30-day schedule.
  • Sign-in sessions: 12 hours; our record of each session is deleted about 30 days after it expires or you sign out. Cookies used during Google sign-in or when opening an invitation: 10 minutes.
  • Links in emails: email verification and password reset links expire after 30 minutes; invitation links after 7 days.
  • Requests to join: an unanswered request expires after 30 days. Once an owner approves or declines it, your name, email and note are removed straight away, and the rest of the record is deleted after 30 days (the clean-up runs weekly, so up to about 37 days).
  • Email records: our email queue keeps a record of each email it sends. For sign-up, password-reset and pairing emails this includes the recipient’s email address; for invitations it is an encrypted copy. The recipient’s address and any email-verification, password-reset or invitation link in the email are removed from that record 7 days after the email is sent (or skipped because it was no longer needed), or 30 days after we stop trying to deliver it. An invitation’s encrypted copy is never removed while the invitation can still be used. The rest of the record (the type of email, whether it was sent, and when) is kept as an operational record without the address. Our database provider’s backups can still contain the address for up to 1 day after it is removed. Records that were already older than this when the rule started are cleared over the following hours and days, a limited number each hour.
  • Unfinished sign-ups: if you start signing up with a password but don’t confirm your email, we keep your email address, name and password hash. They are not deleted automatically at the moment; ask us to remove them (see Contact).
  • Backups: our database provider keeps point-in-time backups for up to 1 day, so deleted information can remain in a backup for up to 1 day after it is removed. If we ever restore from a backup, we re-apply any deletions made after the backup point before the restored data goes live, where the source database survives. If the source database itself is destroyed, that re-application is not yet possible.

Cookies and browser storage

We use only what the app needs to work:

  • randori_session keeps you signed in. It can’t be read by scripts, is sent only over HTTPS on the live site, and lasts 12 hours.
  • Two short-lived cookies are used while you sign in with Google and when you open an invitation. They last 10 minutes.
  • Your browser’s local storage keeps your theme, which tips you have dismissed and where you were in the app. It also keeps a copy of your basic profile, including your email address (removed when you sign out); code drafts (removed when you sign out); unsent shared-editor edits (kept for up to 90 days); whiteboard drafts (no expiry, and they stay after you sign out); and two random identifiers that help the shared editor and video calls tell browsers apart (randori-crdt-client-v1 and randori-peer-id), and a few settings, such as your reminder choice. While you accept an invitation or confirm an account change, your browser’s session storage briefly keeps a reference to it. You can clear all of this at any time in your browser settings.

There are no advertising or analytics cookies. The only third-party script that loads on every visit to the app is Sentry’s error-reporting script (see Service providers).

Your choices and rights

  • See and correct your details with Edit profile in the account menu. You can also change your availability on the Pairing page. To stop pairing, session-time and reminder emails (and, for owners, notices about requests to join), untick Email under Email reminders in Edit profile. Emails you need for your account, such as password resets, are still sent.
  • Leave a circle from the Circle page.
  • Sign out everywhere from the account menu if you think someone else has used your account.
  • Delete your account. Open Edit profile from the account menu and choose Delete account. You confirm it’s you with your password or Google (a sign-in from the last 10 minutes), type DELETE, and confirm once more. Your account is deleted straight away and you are signed out on every device. We delete or anonymise your name, email address, password, Google sign-in link, sessions, availability, email and reminder settings, emails still waiting to be sent to you, invitations you sent that nobody has used, your solo practice runs, the code of your pair runs and the text of your chat messages. Your partners’ history (past pairings, agreed times, completed sessions and recaps) stays, with your name shown as “Deleted member”, and shared pair workspaces stay with your partner until they expire after 90 days. If you are the only owner of a circle, make another member an owner (or archive the circle) first; if you are the only app admin, make another member an app admin first. You can join again later with the same email as a new account. If you can no longer sign in, or would rather ask us, email festusomole14@gmail.com or ask the owner of your circle (see Contact): the person who runs Randori will respond within one month and delete your account and the personal information linked to it, unless the law requires us to keep something.

Under UK data protection law you can also ask for a copy of your information, ask us to correct, delete or restrict it, object to how we use it, or ask for it in a portable format. We use your information because it is needed to provide the service you signed up for, and because we have a legitimate interest in keeping it secure and working. Optional AI feedback would rely on your consent.

If you are unhappy with how we handle your information, please tell us first. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

Security

Randori uses HTTPS throughout, keeps your sign-in cookie out of reach of scripts and stores passwords as bcrypt hashes. Password-reset, email-verification and invitation links are stored as one-way hashes (our email queue also keeps an encrypted copy so it can send or retry the email, and later removes it; see How long we keep it), and they stop working once they expire or are used. The server checks that you are allowed to see each thing you ask for. Code runs in an isolated runner. No system is perfectly secure, so please use a strong password and tell us if you notice anything wrong.
